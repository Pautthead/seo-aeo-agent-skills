**Status vocabulary**

- **VERIFIED** — canonical URL found, resolves, and contains the attributed claim. Numbers copied from the source.
- **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — source is real and the core claim holds, but a specific number, date or framing is wrong or unsupported.
- **NOT FOUND** — no canonical source located, or the attributed number does not appear in the source. Treat as unusable.
- **CONTRADICTED** — the source says something different from what we attribute to it.

**Vendor-marked sources** are labelled. A vendor source is not automatically wrong; it is simply a commercial interest whose published methodology may be incomplete.

### Anthropic — DOES **NOT** publish exact strings ❌
Source: **`https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler`** (updated **2026-04-07**; now also served from `https://support.claude.com/`).

The article documents exactly three bots — `ClaudeBot` (training), `Claude-User` (user-initiated), `Claude-SearchBot` (search indexing) — with a "what happens when you disable it" column, robots.txt examples, `Crawl-delay` support, and an IP-range list. **It does not print a single full User-Agent header.** Third-party directories actively disagree: some list `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClaudeBot/1.0; +claudebot@anthropic.com)`, others `Mozilla/5.0 AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko; compatible; ClaudeBot/1.0; +claudebot@anthropic.com)`, and they disagree on whether `Claude-SearchBot` ends in an email or a URL (one commonly-cited URL, `anthropic.com/claudesearchbot`, returns 404).

**Consequences for `CRAWLERS.md`:**

1. The Anthropic block headed **"Exact UA strings"** must be retitled. These are community-observed, not vendor-published. Relabel confidence from `vendor-documented` to **observed, version drift expected**.
2. The line *"All three share the verification address `claudebot@anthropic.com`"* is **probably wrong** for at least `Claude-User` and `Claude-SearchBot`. Delete the claim, or state it as contested.
3. The correct verification method is the **published IP list**, not the UA string. Multiple sources converge on `https://claude.com/crawling/bots.json`. Note that a UA header is a claim anyone can make — the repo already says this well for the email address; extend it to the whole string.
4. A **fourth** Anthropic agent, `claude-code`, exists in the wild (e.g. `claude-code/2.1.131 (cli)`) and is absent from the repo's table. Per Ahrefs' llms.txt data, `Claude-Code` was the **second-largest** AI fetcher of llms.txt after GPTBot — it is not a rounding error.

## Net position

The core thesis survives this audit comfortably. **The "llms.txt does nothing" evidence base is real, and five of the six studies verify essentially word-for-word** — Ahrefs, SE Ranking, CueScout, OtterlyAI and Search Engine Land all reproduce their numbers exactly, and the Zenodo nuance and the server-log study are both genuine. The information-gain patent is real, correctly characterised, and correctly attributed. arXiv 2605.25517 verifies completely. The GitHub comparison table is factually accurate.

What did not survive: **one fabricated quote (Danny Sullivan), one wrong number attached to a real study (2,135 robots.txt requests), one wrong metric label (98% accuracy for 97.0 macro-F1), two broken URLs that silently resolve to index pages, one source that cannot be found at all (ProCloser), and a family of User-Agent strings that were presented as vendor-exact when two of the four vendors do not publish them — including two outright wrong version numbers in copy-pasteable curl commands.**

That is a good result for a repo whose thesis is traceability. The failures are concentrated in exactly the place a reviewer would look hardest: the citation apparatus. Fix items 1–14 and the evidence base is defensible end to end.
